From paper to play

Crayon in.
Adventure out.

A drawing, a spoken idea, and one tap. That's the entire input. Underneath, four steps turn it into a playable, safety-checked chapter — in about the time it takes to sharpen a pencil.

4-step pipeline Voice first Checked before it plays Printable ending

The pipeline

Look. Listen.
Think. Play.

Every chapter runs through the same four stages. The kid only ever sees the last one — a story that knows their drawing by name.

01

Look

A photo of the drawing goes in. The vision model picks out the character: shape, colour, mood, the extra leg nobody mentioned.

vision · ~500 tokens
02

Listen

Your kid says what happens. Speech becomes text on the device, so nobody has to type or read a single word.

speech-to-text · local
03

Think

Gemini 4 writes the next short chapter, then reviews it in thinking mode against the child-safety brief before a word is spoken.

gemini 4 · guardrail
04

Play

Narration, two clear choices, and the next beat. At the end: a printable colouring page and one question for the table.

narration · print

Inside the story engine

Six small machines.
One very big imagination.

The technical bits, for the grown-ups who like to know what's under the hood. None of it is visible to a four-year-old, which is exactly the point.

The story brain

Holds the world, the lesson and your kid's drawing in mind, then writes one short chapter at a time — never the whole book.

Gemini 4 · thinking mode

Drawing eyes

Reads crayon characters as they are — a blob, a dragon, a cat in a hat — without needing a perfect photo or a tidy background.

Vision encoder

The safety pass

A second reasoning pass checks each chapter for kindness, age-fit and scare-factor. If it fails, the chapter is rewritten before it plays.

Guardrail · child-safety brief

Voice in, voice out

Speech-to-text and narration run on the device. Pre-readers never touch a keyboard, and the story talks back in a warm, unhurried voice.

Whisper + Piper · local

Choices that remember

Each pick updates the world state: who's a friend now, what's in the bag, how the hero feels. Chapter three knows what happened in chapter one.

Branch memory · story state

The fridge-door ending

Every adventure finishes with a printable colouring page of your kid's own character and one question to talk about at dinner.

SVG export · print

Built for real life

Made to be
interrupted.

Small kids don't do forty-minute sessions. They do four minutes, a snack, a wobble, and then maybe some more. StorySpark is shaped around that.

  • ✓

    Chapters, not marathons

    Each beat is 2–3 minutes. There's always a clean place to stop, and “the end” is never far away.

  • ✓

    Pick up where you left off

    The story remembers the drawing, the choices and the lesson. Come back after lunch and keep going.

  • ✓

    Replay is the feature

    Same world, different choice, different ending. Replaying is how kids test the lesson from the other side.
