A lesson in every adventure.

StorySpark is a library of twelve short story worlds for kids aged 4–10. Each one hands your child a problem, two or three real choices, and a lesson they discover by doing — not by being told.

The learning loop

Listen. Choose.
Notice. Tell it back.

Kids don't learn a lesson because a narrator says it slowly. They learn it because they made a decision and felt what happened next. StorySpark builds that loop into every chapter.

1

Listen

A warm narrator reads the chapter aloud, folding your child's own words and drawing into the story.

2

Choose

Two or three clear options, always spoken and pictured. No reading, no timer, no way to get it “wrong”.

3

Notice

The story names the feeling out loud: “Pip's chest feels tight. That's nervous.” Feelings get words.

4

Tell it back

One gentle question at the end invites a retelling — the part where the lesson actually sticks.

Under the story

A lesson, disguised as a game.

Here's how a single story beat turns into practice. The child sees a firefly. We see a decision about whether to help.

Three story beats, three lessons
Story moment What the kid does Lesson they meet
A firefly is tangled
Pip could help, fetch help, or share a light.		 Picks a first move, then lives with the outcome and can replay it differently. Kindness has more than one shape — and asking for help is one of them.
The knight hears snoring
The door is enormous; the knight is tiny.		 Chooses one small step instead of a whole plan. Then takes the next one. Courage is usually just small steps standing in a row.
The last swing is taken
Three kids want the same four minutes.		 Splits time, invents a rule, or finds a game that fits everyone. Fairness isn't a rule you're given. It's something you build together.
